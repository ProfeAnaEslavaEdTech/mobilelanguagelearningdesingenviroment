# Theoretical Framework
## Innovative Instructional Design & Competency Development

> © 2026 Ana Eslava-Graterol (ProfeAnaEslavaEdTech). All Rights Reserved. · Proprietary — see [LICENSE](../LICENSE)

This document captures the theoretical framework underpinning the mobile
learning design tool for language teaching. It is a faithful transcription of
the source mind map, organised into four pillars. Three of the pillars are
pedagogical **approaches** (GenAI Competency, G-CLIL & Academic Writing, and
CTL); together they inform the fourth, the **Mobile Learning Design** delivery

internationally-unified competences and modes of communication (defined by the
**Council of Europe**) that make MLLDE replicable across every language; for
Spanish content, Language Passport draws on the **Plan curricular del Instituto
Cervantes**.

## 0. The competence core (CEFR / Instituto Cervantes) — what every lesson develops

The **CEFR** (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages), developed by
the **Council of Europe**, replaces the traditional four skills (reading, writing,
listening, speaking) with **four dynamic modes of communication**, powered by a
core block of **three communicative language competences**. This is the standard
that makes MLLDE **replicable across all languages**: CEFR supplies the
internationally-unified *what* and *how far* (leveled can-do targets, A1–C2,
grouped into Basic / Independent / Proficient User); MLLDE supplies the *approach*
(the how). **Every MLLDE lesson or level plan is specified against this core.**
For a given language, the CEFR-aligned *content* comes from that language's
official reference — **for Spanish, Language Passport uses the *Plan curricular del
Instituto Cervantes*** (Instituto Cervantes, the official institution for teaching
Spanish). CEFR © Council of Europe; the Plan curricular © Instituto Cervantes.

### The four modes of communication
- **Reception** — receiving and processing spoken or written language without
  producing a new message (e.g. reading a novel, listening to a podcast).
- **Production** — constructing and delivering a continuous text to one or more
  silent listeners/readers (e.g. a public speech, an essay).
- **Interaction** — a joint exchange where parties alternate reception and
  production (e.g. conversation, debate, an online chat thread).
- **Mediation** — acting as a social agent to bridge gaps between people, texts
  or cultures (e.g. translating a document, summarising a meeting, resolving a
  cultural misunderstanding).

### The three communicative language competences

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           COMMUNICATIVE LANGUAGE COMPETENCES           │
└───────┬───────────────────┬────────────────────┬──────┘
        │                   │                    │
┌───────▼───────┐   ┌───────▼───────┐    ┌───────▼───────┐
│  Linguistic   │   │Sociolinguistic│    │   Pragmatic   │
│  (Grammar /   │   │  (Cultural /  │    │  (Function /  │
│  Vocabulary)  │   │  Politeness)  │    │  Coherence)   │
└───────────────┘   └───────────────┘    └───────────────┘
```

- **Linguistic** — knowledge of the formal system: vocabulary, grammar, syntax,
  phonology, spelling.
- **Sociolinguistic** — adapting language to social context: politeness, register
  (formal vs. informal), idioms, cultural references.
- **Pragmatic** — functional use: structuring coherent discourse, organising ideas
  logically, tone/irony — getting things done.

### Summary matrix — modes × competences

| Mode | Linguistic focus | Sociolinguistic focus | Pragmatic focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reception** | understanding complex sentence syntax | identifying regional accents / registers | grasping overall tone & author intent |
| **Production** | accurate vocabulary in a presentation | adapting speech level to a formal audience | structuring a talk with transition words |
| **Interaction** | correct verb tenses in a debate | appropriate conversational fillers | managing turn-taking smoothly during chat |
| **Mediation** | translating precise terminology for others | explaining taboos across cultures | simplifying a complex manual into a summary |

### How MLLDE / Language Passport delivers the core

| Mode | Where it lives in a lesson today | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Reception** | model dialogue (listen + read); audio | ✅ |
| **Production** | reflection — Speak / Write (monologue) | ✅ |
| **Interaction** | the mission (order with the *camarero*); Grace | ✅ |
| **Mediation** | *relay / explain / simplify a text or concept for someone else* | 🔒 **B2 · C1 · C2** — introduced at upper levels (not required at A1–A2) |

**Level note.** In MLLDE the three modes **Reception · Production · Interaction**
anchor every level from A1 upward; the **Mediation** mode is introduced from
**B2** onward (B2 · C1 · C2), where the learner can *relay, explain and simplify a
text or concept for someone else* (translate a menu for a friend, summarise what
the waiter said, explain a cultural custom). *(CEFR also defines simpler mediation
at lower levels; MLLDE positions the mediation* mode *as an upper-level
competence.)*

Competence mapping in a lesson: **Linguistic** → vocabulary, chunks, gap-fill,
grammar (rules + grammar book + Grace); **Sociolinguistic** → the culture &
pragmatics cards (register, *nos pone…* vs *quiero*); **Pragmatic** → the
mission's polite, complete order and the coherence of the reflection.

> **Planning rule (standing).** Every lesson/level plan states, **per mode**,
> which **competences** (Linguistic · Sociolinguistic · Pragmatic) it builds, and
> the **CEFR level** it targets. **Reception · Production · Interaction** apply at
> every level (A1+); **Mediation** applies from **B2** upward. Competence =
> *what*; mode = *how it is used*; level = *how far*; MLLDE = *the method*.

## 1. GenAI Competency (CMU Study)

Evidence base for building generative-AI competency, drawn from the Carnegie
Mellon University study.

### Competencies Measured
- **Knowledge of LLM Mechanics** — understanding how large language models work.
- **Prompt Engineering Skills** — crafting effective prompts.
- **Fact and Source Checking** — verifying model output against reliable sources.
- **Self-Efficacy** — learner confidence in using GenAI tools.

### Intervention Design
- **Asynchronous Online Modules** — self-paced delivery.
- **Learn-by-Doing Practice** — active, hands-on tasks.
- **Immediate Explanatory Feedback** — feedback that explains, not just corrects.

### Outcomes
- **Significant Prompting Improvement** — measurable gains in prompt quality.
- **Increased Self-Confidence** — higher learner self-efficacy.
- **Equitable Demographic Impact** — benefits distributed across demographics.

## 2. Mobile Learning Design

The delivery layer: principles and strategies for designing language-learning
experiences on mobile devices. Grounded in Clothier, P. (2026), *Mastering
Mobile Learning Design: A Practical Guide* (1st ed.), Routledge.

### Core Principles
- **Context-Driven Design** — design for the learner's real usage context.
- **Brevity and Scanability** — short, easily scanned content.
- **Learner Empathy** — centre the learner's needs and constraints.

### Instructional Strategies
- **Microlearning** — small, focused learning units.
- **Storytelling** — narrative to carry content.
- **Gamification** — game mechanics to drive engagement.
- **Performance Support** — just-in-time help at the point of need.

### Future Trends
- **AI-Assisted Development** — AI in the authoring/design process.
- **Adaptive Learning Paths** — personalised, responsive sequencing.

## 3. G-CLIL and Academic Writing

Gamified Content and Language Integrated Learning applied to academic writing.

### G-CLIL Framework
- **Gamification Ring Architecture** — layered gamification structure.
- **CLIL 4Cs Integration** — Content, Communication, Cognition, Culture.
- **Disciplinary Identity Storyline** — narrative anchored in disciplinary identity.

### Learning Cycle (IPO)
- **Input (Exposure)** — exposure to target content and language.
- **Processing (Practice)** — guided practice and manipulation.
- **Output (Genre Production)** — producing the target academic genre.

### Design Methodology (SAM — Successive Approximation Model)
- **Preparation Phase** — gather information and background.
- **Iterative Design (Savvy Start)** — collaborative iterative design kickoff.
- **Iterative Development (Build Cycles)** — build, review, refine in cycles.

## 4. CTL Framework

Contextual Teaching and Learning — connecting content to meaningful, real-world
context.

### Core Components
- **Meaningful Connections** — link learning to lived context.
- **Significant Work** — tasks with real value.
- **Self-Regulated Learning** — learner ownership of the process.
- **Authentic Assessment** — assessment grounded in real performance.

### Evolution Phases
- **Philosophical Roots (Dewey)** — experiential-education origins.
- **Constructivism (Vygotsky)** — social construction of knowledge.
- **Federal Formalization (1990s)** — formal adoption in the 1990s.

### REACT Strategies
- **Relating and Experiencing** — connect to prior experience; hands-on.
- **Applying and Cooperating** — apply knowledge; collaborate.
- **Transferring** — transfer learning to new contexts.

## How the pillars relate

- **GenAI Competency**, **G-CLIL & Academic Writing**, and **CTL** are the three
  pedagogical approaches.
- **Mobile Learning Design** is the delivery layer through which the three
  approaches are operationalised for language teaching on mobile devices.
- Shared threads across the approaches: gamification (Mobile Learning Design ↔
  G-CLIL), learn-by-doing / experiential learning (GenAI ↔ CTL), and iterative,
  learner-centred design (SAM ↔ Core Principles ↔ Self-Regulated Learning).
