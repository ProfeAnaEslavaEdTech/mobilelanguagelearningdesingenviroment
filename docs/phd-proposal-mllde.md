## 1. Abstract

Mainstream mobile language-learning applications achieve high engagement but are
frequently criticised for privileging decontextualised vocabulary recognition over
**communicative and pragmatic competence**, and for lacking an explicit,
research-grounded design rationale that teachers can adapt. This project proposes and
empirically evaluates the **Mobile Language Learning Design Environment (MLLDE)**: an
original instructional- and curriculum-design framework that synthesises established
theories — mobile learning design (Clothier), Gamified CLIL and the Input–Processing–
Output cycle (Casañ-Pitarch), the CLIL 4Cs (Coyle), Engage–Study–Activate (Harmer),
Contextual Teaching and Learning / REACT (Johnson; CORD), multimedia/dual-coding
(Mayer), the Lexical Approach (Lewis), and interlanguage pragmatics (Kasper & Rose) —
into a coherent method for **short-form mobile lessons (typically 5–15 minutes)** that satisfy
mobile UX constraints and integrate responsible human–AI tutoring. The framework is
instantiated in **Language Passport**, a gamified “travel-quest” prototype (already
built as a proof of concept). Using **Design-Based Research** operationalised through
the **Successive Approximation Model**, the study will iteratively refine MLLDE and
measure its effects on learners’ communicative/pragmatic performance, self-efficacy,
engagement, and active recall, and will assess the framework’s **replicability** across
destinations and target languages. The intended contributions are a validated design
framework, a set of empirically grounded design principles, an open authoring method
that lets educators create lessons without programming, and evidence for the
application of languages research to educational technology.

## 2. Introduction and rationale

Smartphones are now the primary gateway to informal language study, yet the dominant
apps optimise for streaks and retention metrics more than for the ability to *act* in
the language in authentic situations. Teachers and applied linguists, meanwhile, hold
robust pedagogical theory but lack a fast, principled way to translate it into engaging
mobile experiences. This gap — between **sound second-language pedagogy** and
**effective mobile UX** — motivates the present research.

The applicant’s prior work (an MA thesis on disciplinary variation in metadiscourse,
UPV, Department of Applied Linguistics) and professional practice in instructional
design converge on a single question: *how should mobile language lessons be designed
so that they are simultaneously pedagogically rigorous, motivating, and buildable by
educators?* MLLDE is the proposed answer, and *Language Passport* is its first working
instantiation.

## 3. State of the art

- **Mobile-assisted language learning (MALL).** MALL research documents affordances of
  ubiquity, personalisation, and micro-sessions, but also fragmentation and shallow
  practice (Kukulska-Hulme & Shield, 2008; Stockwell, 2010; Godwin-Jones, 2011).
- **Microlearning & mobile learning design.** Clothier (2026) argues mobile learning
  needs its *own* design logic — context-driven, cognitive-load-managed, Bloom-based
  objectives — rather than shrunken e-learning.
- **Gamification & serious games.** Gamification can increase motivation when mechanics
  are tied to learning goals (Deterding, Dixon, Khaled & Nacke, 2011); Casañ-Pitarch’s
  **G-CLIL** four-ring model binds game mechanics to disciplinary language tasks.
- **CLIL & communicative/task-based pedagogy.** Coyle, Hood & Marsh (2010) (4Cs);
  Harmer (2007) (ESA) provide content-and-communication and sequencing logics.
- **Contextual & authentic assessment.** Johnson (2002) and the REACT strategies
  (CORD; Crawford, 2001) foreground meaningful, performance-based learning.
- **Cognition & lexis.** Mayer (2009) (multimedia/dual-coding); Lewis (1993) (lexical
  chunks) inform vocabulary and hinting design.
- **Pragmatics.** Interlanguage-pragmatics research (Kasper & Rose, 2001) shows
  pragmatic competence is teachable yet under-served by apps.
- **Generative AI tutoring.** Emerging work explores LLM tutors; a key open problem is
  **scaffolding without giving answers** (responsible human–AI collaboration).

**Synthesis gap.** These strands are rarely integrated into one design method built
*for* the short-form mobile constraint, nor evaluated as a replicable, teacher-
authorable environment. MLLDE addresses this gap.

## 4. Research questions and hypotheses

- **RQ1 (design).** What design principles constitute an effective MLLDE for 5–15-minute
  mobile language lessons that integrate mobile UX, CLIL/IPO pedagogy, gamification, and
  AI-mentor scaffolding?
- **RQ2 (outcomes).** How does a MLLDE-designed lesson (*Language Passport*) affect
  learners’ **communicative and pragmatic task performance** and **self-efficacy**
  compared with a baseline condition?
- **RQ3 (mechanisms).** How do (a) gamified authentic assessment, (b) AI-mentor graded
  hints, and (c) dual-coded **animated hints** influence engagement and active recall?
- **RQ4 (transferability).** To what extent is the MLLDE method **replicable** across
  destinations and target languages via an *engine + data* authoring model?

*Hypotheses (illustrative):* H1 — MLLDE lessons yield higher gains in an authentic
speaking/ordering task than a vocabulary-drill baseline; H2 — the animated-hint
(same-image) condition improves recall and reduces cognitive load versus a text-hint
condition; H3 — AI-mentor scaffolding that withholds answers increases learner
production over an answer-giving condition.

---

## 5. Aims and objectives

**General aim.** To develop, formalise, and empirically validate the MLLDE framework
and its design principles for gamified micro-lessons in mobile language learning.

**Specific objectives.**
1. Formalise MLLDE (constructs, layers, the Destination Schema) from the theory
   synthesis. *(RQ1)*
2. Build and iteratively refine *Language Passport* as the design artifact. *(RQ1)*
3. Measure learning outcomes, self-efficacy, engagement, and usability. *(RQ2–RQ3)*
4. Isolate the effect of key mechanisms (animated hints; AI scaffolding; authentic
   assessment) through focused comparisons. *(RQ3)*
5. Test replicability by authoring additional destinations/languages and evaluating
   design effort and fidelity. *(RQ4)*
6. Produce validated design guidelines and an authoring method for educators.

## 6. Theoretical framework

MLLDE nests established frameworks rather than treating them as alternatives:

```
SAM  ── how the artifact is BUILT (agile; Preparation · Savvy Start · Alpha/Beta/Gold)
 └─ G-CLIL Four Rings ── how the lesson is STRUCTURED (mechanics = language tasks)
     └─ IPO × ESA ── how each micro-session FLOWS (input → processing → output)
         └─ CTL + REACT ── how learning stays MEANINGFUL & AUTHENTIC
             └─ Mobile UX (Clothier) + multimedia/dual-coding (Mayer) + pragmatics
                + responsible human–AI collaboration ── how it FEELS and TUTORS on a phone
```

The synthesis is the study’s theoretical contribution; the individual frameworks remain
attributed to their authors.

## 7. Methodology

**Design.** **Design-Based Research (DBR)** (Reeves; Anderson & Shattuck, 2012):
iterative development and study of a design intervention in authentic contexts to
produce both a working artifact and transferable design theory. Build cycles follow the
**Successive Approximation Model** (Allen & Sites, 2012): Preparation → Iterative Design
(Savvy Start) → Iterative Development (Alpha/Beta/Gold).

**Phases.**
1. **Analysis & design** — formalise MLLDE; expert validation of the framework and the
   Destination Schema (Delphi / expert panel of applied linguists and designers).
2. **Development** — refine *Language Passport* (already prototyped: Barcelona, Sevilla;
   Grace AI mentor).
3. **Iterative evaluation** — pilot → alpha → beta cycles with learners.
4. **Replication study** — author new destinations/languages; measure design effort and
   pedagogical fidelity.

**Participants.** Adult A1–A2 learners of Spanish (and, in the replication phase, of a
second target language), recruited via UPV language services / partner language schools;
plus an expert panel. Sample sizes and sampling to be finalised with the supervisor and
ethics board.

**Design comparisons (RQ3).** Within-/between-subjects micro-experiments contrasting:
animated-image hints vs. text hints; AI scaffolding vs. answer-giving; authentic-mission
assessment vs. multiple-choice.

**Instruments & data.**
- **Performance:** CEFR-aligned **speaking/ordering task** rubric; a written-production
  task; pragmatic-appropriateness ratings.
- **Affective:** language-learning **self-efficacy** and motivation scales.
- **Engagement/behaviour:** app telemetry (completion, coins, retries, time-on-task).
- **Usability:** System Usability Scale (SUS); cognitive-load measures.
- **Qualitative:** semi-structured interviews; think-aloud; expert reviews.

**Analysis.** Mixed methods — inferential statistics (pre/post, between-condition
effects) triangulated with thematic analysis of qualitative data.

**Rigour.** Iterative member-checking, expert validation, inter-rater reliability on
rubrics, and DBR’s emphasis on ecological validity.

## 10. Relevance and fit with the doctoral programme

The project sits squarely within the **Grupo de Investigación para la Enseñanza de
Lenguas Asistida por Ordenador** (Computer-Assisted Language Teaching research group)
and its two research lines: **Aprendizaje de lenguas asistido por ordenador (CALL)** and
**E-language learning**. Mobile-assisted language learning (MALL) — the terrain of
*Language Passport* — is a core, contemporary sub-field of CALL, and the study’s
gamified, AI-mediated, mobile micro-lessons are a direct instance of e-language
learning.

More broadly, the programme’s focus on **languages, literatures and cultures *and their
applications*** encompasses applied-linguistics research that produces usable artefacts
and methods. MLLDE contributes to that *applications* strand: it applies
second-language-acquisition and CLIL theory, discourse/pragmatics, and instructional
design to a real technological product — aligning with the CALL group’s expertise, the
Department of Applied Linguistics, and the applicant’s prior UPV training.

## 11. Ethical considerations

Informed consent; voluntary participation; data minimisation and GDPR compliance
(pseudonymised telemetry; client-side storage where possible); safeguarding in any
AI-mediated interaction (the AI mentor is constrained to educational, age-appropriate
tutoring). Ethics approval will be sought from the UPV committee before data collection.
Third-party pedagogical frameworks are credited to their authors; the applicant’s
original contributions are protected (proprietary licence; “Language Passport”™).

## 12. Preliminary results (proof of concept)

A working prototype already evidences feasibility: *Language Passport* runs the full
MLLDE lesson cycle (arrival → animated vocabulary → lexical chunks → model dialogue →
gap-fill → authentic NPC mission → oral/written reflection → culture & pragmatics cards
→ rewards), with a persistent gamified economy, a world-tour map, offline PWA delivery,
and an AI game master (**Grace**, deployed as a custom GPT). Two destinations (Barcelona,
Sevilla) are playable, demonstrating the *engine + data* replicability the study will
test. *(A “before/after” redesign and the full design record are documented in the
project repository.)*
